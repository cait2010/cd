San Francisco Luxury Waterfront Homes Available Now in 2026

San Francisco waterfront luxury homes on the market right now

This is a live roundup of San Francisco luxury property listings on the water — and, specifically, the homes that are ready to move into. It is not a broad market overview, and it is not a full inventory dump. The aim is narrower: which waterfront homes are genuinely worth a tour right now, and which move-in-ready home stands out most clearly among them.

Waterfront in this city is not one market. The Marina reads differently from South Beach, and both read differently from Yerba Buena Island, where the housing is newer and the price range sits well below the trophy stretches. Search sites reflect that spread — Zillow's waterfront filter currently returns 16 results ranging from 675 Marina Blvd at $8,500,000 down to a one-bedroom at 1 Bristol Ct on Yerba Buena Island at $749,000. Redfin's version of the same search shows 15 homes, and Sotheby's waterfront lifestyle view surfaces only four, including 1 Steuart Lane #1103 in South Beach at $3,195,000.

Those portals are good at surfacing inventory and less useful on the parts that decide whether you'd actually live there: view corridors that neighboring development can close, fog and wind exposure by block, HOA rules on rentals and renovation, flood and sea-level considerations, and how much work a home really needs. This roundup separates true waterfront prestige homes from listings that are merely near the water.

Luxury waterfront homes available now, with the neighborhoods that shape value

Waterfront supply in San Francisco stays sparse. A waterfront search on Zillow returns 16 results, and Redfin's waterfront filter for the city shows 15 homes. Those counts tell you the category is small — they don't tell you which of those homes actually sits on the water, faces the right direction, or lives well on a windy Tuesday in July. That's the gap worth closing before you tour anything.

Four listings currently on the market show how much the pocket matters:

  • 675 Marina Blvd (Marina) — priced at $8,500,000 with 4 bedrooms, 5 baths, and 4,016 square feet, per both Zillow and Redfin. This is the closest thing the city offers to a front-row seat: Marina Green, the Bay, and the Golden Gate in one frame. It's also the block where wind and fog microclimates are most obvious, so a second visit at a different hour is worth the trouble.
  • 1 Steuart Lane #1103 (South Beach) — listed at $3,195,000 on Sotheby's waterfront lifestyle search. Embarcadero-adjacent new construction trades some of the Marina's raw view drama for elevator access, service, and a walkable stretch of ferry terminal, ballpark, and restaurants. Buyers who want move-in-ready condition usually land here.
  • 1 Bristol Ct #205 (Yerba Buena Island) — $749,000 for 1 bedroom, 1 bath, and 930 square feet, which is the entry point in the waterfront category and a reminder that "waterfront" spans an enormous price band. Island living means bridge access, a young HOA structure, and a skyline view most mainland pockets can't match. Read the association documents closely.
  • 265 Moncada Way — $3,895,000, also surfaced in the Sotheby's lifestyle view. It's a useful example of a home marketed under a waterfront lifestyle banner rather than sitting directly on the shoreline. Nothing wrong with that, but it's a different buyer case than 675 Marina Blvd, and the search filters won't flag the difference for you.

That distinction — true waterfront prestige versus near-water inventory — is the single most useful screen when reviewing San Francisco luxury property listings. Portals index location; they don't index exposure, view corridor protection, HOA restrictions on renovations, or how a given block handles king tides.

When comparing Marina, South Beach, and Yerba Buena Island, most buyers notice the same three things: how much sun the unit gets, how far they have to walk for groceries, and how quickly they can get out of the city. Weigh those honestly before price.

The move-in-ready waterfront home that stands out most clearly

Among current waterfront inventory, the strongest move-in-ready pick is 1 Steuart Lane #1103 in South Beach, listed at $3,195,000. It appears on Sotheby's International Realty's waterfront lifestyle search, one of only four San Francisco homes surfaced there, and it is the clearest example of a finished, newer-construction residence on the water rather than a renovation project dressed up as one.

The reasoning is straightforward. A buyer who wants to close and move in is buying certainty: modern building systems, elevator and garage access already in place, finishes that do not need to be pulled out, and a bathroom and kitchen that will still read as current in five years. In older Marina and Sea Cliff stock, that same standard usually means permits, seismic and electrical work, and months of displacement. Here the post-closing punch list is realistically furniture, window coverings, and a security or audio-visual install.

Notably, this is not the priciest option available. The headline waterfront number in San Francisco luxury property listings right now is 675 Marina Blvd at $8,500,000 — 4 bedrooms, 5 baths, 4,016 square feet — which both Zillow and Redfin feature at the top of their waterfront results. That home is a trophy address on the northern shoreline. But for a buyer weighing certainty against outlay, a finished South Beach residence at roughly a third of that price removes renovation risk entirely.

The honest tradeoffs: this is a condominium, so monthly dues and building rules govern rentals, pets, and remodeling, and those documents deserve a careful read before contingencies come off. View corridors also vary sharply floor to floor and by exposure — the Bay Bridge and waterfront outlook from a mid-rise unit is not the same asset as an unobstructable one. Confirm the current price, dues, and reserve study directly with the listing agent.

What to check before touring a waterfront luxury home in San Francisco

Waterfront inventory in the city is thin — the major portals were showing roughly 15 to 16 active waterfront results at last check, from a $749,000 one-bedroom on Yerba Buena Island to an $8.5 million four-bedroom on Marina Boulevard. With a set that small, the difference between a good buy and a costly one usually comes down to what you verify before you walk in.

View corridors. Ask what protects the view. A sightline over the Bay across a public park, a street grid, or a height-limited district behaves very differently from one that crosses a soft-site parcel or a low building on a lot zoned taller. Pull the neighboring parcels' zoning and any filed project applications with the San Francisco Planning Department, and see the unit at the time of day you would actually use it. Partial views get priced like full ones surprisingly often.

HOA rules and carrying costs. In waterfront condo buildings, the governing documents decide more than the floor plan does. Read the rules on renovation scope and approval timelines, window and facade alterations, short-term rentals, pets, and rental caps. Then look at the reserve study, any special assessments, and pending litigation. Monthly dues on newer towers along the waterfront can move a buyer's real cost meaningfully above the mortgage math.

Flood and water exposure. Check the property's specific position — FEMA's flood map service and the city's sea level rise guidance are both public — rather than assuming a whole neighborhood shares one risk profile. It affects insurance pricing, lender requirements, and resale.

Renovation level. Separate cosmetic from structural honestly. Fresh paint and a new kitchen are not the same as a re-clad building envelope, updated electrical and plumbing, seismic retrofit, or window replacement on a salt-air exposure. Ask for permit history.

Exposure and microclimates. Fog, wind, and traffic noise shift block by block here. A Marina-facing unit and a South Beach unit are not the same experience in July.

That block-level read is what separates genuinely comparable San Francisco luxury property listings from ones that only look alike online — and it is worth an honest assessment before an offer, not after.

Frequently asked questions about San Francisco waterfront luxury listings

Are waterfront luxury homes actually available right now?

Yes, but the pool is small and it turns over quickly. When this roundup was compiled, the major portals were each showing roughly a dozen to sixteen waterfront-tagged homes across the city — Zillow's San Francisco County waterfront search and its citywide equivalent listed 16 results, Redfin showed 15, and Sotheby's International Realty's waterfront lifestyle view surfaced just 4 San Francisco homes. That spread tells you two things: the search filters disagree, and true waterfront prestige property is a narrow slice of the city's high-end inventory. A single sale in Marina or South Beach can meaningfully change what's left.

What price range should I plan for?

Wider than most buyers expect. At the accessible end, 1 Bristol Ct #205 on Yerba Buena Island — a 1-bedroom, 1-bath unit of about 930 square feet — appeared at $749,000. In the middle, 1 Steuart Lane #1103 in South Beach was listed at $3,195,000, and 265 Moncada Way at $3,895,000. At the top of the current set, 675 Marina Blvd, a 4-bedroom, 5-bath home of roughly 4,016 square feet, was listed at $8,500,000.

So the practical answer: expect entry-level waterfront condos under $1 million, a deep band of view-corridor condominiums between roughly $3 million and $4 million, and single-family waterfront homes in the eight-figure conversation. Where you land depends less on square footage than on exposure, protected views, HOA rules, and renovation level — the four filters that separate comparable-looking San Francisco luxury property listings by millions.

Is portal data reliable for what's available today?

Treat it as a starting map, not a live feed. Portal counts lag pending status, private and pocket inventory never appears there, and "waterfront" is applied inconsistently — some near-water blocks are tagged in, some genuinely water-facing units are left out. Before you build a shortlist or a competitive offer strategy around a specific address, confirm current status, disclosure package availability, and offer-date timing directly with a licensed agent working the micro market.

How do I request a private showing?

Reach out with the specific addresses or neighborhood pockets you want to see, your target price range, your financing position (pre-approval letter or proof of funds), and your availability window over the next one to two weeks. That set of details lets your representative confirm access quickly, since many high-end waterfront units require advance notice, building approval, or occupied-showing coordination.

It also helps to say what you're solving for — morning light versus afternoon fog, a Marina Green outlook versus a South Beach ferry view, elevator and parking counts, or flood and insurance questions. Those preferences narrow a citywide list to three or four addresses worth your Saturday, and they shape the honest assessment you get back about which buildings hold value.
